## TL;DR

- Canon is not a value judgment or gatekeeping list—it is infrastructure: the shared reference points a medium uses to teach, fund, copy, and argue.
- Games are unusually vulnerable to forgetting (hardware decay, service shutdowns, control obsolescence). Canon becomes a memory scaffold that decides what survives.
- Canon concentrates through network effects: the already-known becomes more knowable, and a handful of titles become discourse hubs.
- Sales ≠ canon. Sales measure adoption; canon measures cultural function ("what did this enable next?").
- Personal preference does not make the question irrelevant—preference itself is shaped by exposure, framing, and scaffolding.

## Outline: Key Points to Develop

### 1. Canon is uncomfortable, but unavoidable

- The word evokes gatekeeping, committees, hierarchies.
- The instinctive rejection ("like what you like") is emotionally healthy but analytically incomplete.
- Canon will form whether we name it or not. The question is whether we treat it as superstition or as a map of forces.

### 2. Canon as infrastructure, not honor roll

- Canon is the medium's shared compression format: the shortlist of objects that survive retelling across time, platforms, and cohorts.
- It is what we reach for when we:
  - Explain games to non-players
  - Justify budgets and greenlight decisions
  - Teach design
  - Recruit talent
  - Argue about what the medium can do
- Canon supplies exemplars, and exemplars steer a medium.

### 3. Games are unusually vulnerable to forgetting

- Hardware ages. Services shut down. Control schemes fall out of fashion. Social contexts evaporate.
- Unlike books or films, many games become literally unplayable within decades.
- Canon becomes a memory scaffold: it tells the next generation what was worth keeping alive.
- What gets preserved shapes what gets copied and funded.

### 4. Canon concentrates through network effects

- Attention is a network: the already-known becomes more knowable.
- Algorithms amplify this. A handful of games become hubs that shape discourse.
- Axis strength (handles, primitives, hinges, mood, honest contracts) helps a game become a good hub—but path dependence and context decide which candidates reach hub status early.
- First-mover and cohort-timing effects are real and should be acknowledged.

### 5. Sales ≠ canon

- Sales measure adoption, not cultural function.
- A game can sell massively and leave little behind except a monetization template.
- Another can sell modestly and alter the medium's vocabulary for decades.
- Canon is closer to "what did this enable next?" than "how many units did it move?"

### 6. Preference is not sealed

- Personal taste feels sovereign, but it is shaped by exposure, framing, community scaffolding, and life circumstances.
- When we can name the axes of appeal (see *[The Elements of a Good and Widely Appealing Game]({{< ref "wide-appeal" >}})*), we gain vocabulary that protects us from mistaking extraction for value and compulsion for conversion.
- We become harder to manipulate with retention proxies because we can say, plainly, what has meaning and what merely captures time.

### 7. A mature discourse holds both truths

- These are the structural reasons a game traveled.
- These are the externalities that amplified it.
- Neither erases the other. Canon is predictable without being morally correct or complete.
