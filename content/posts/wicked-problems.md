## Thesis

(This is about small scale, 4-40 people gamedev)

Advice on what the right thing to do is (in a business organisation context) can be found in abundance. Real problems never are clean and full of contradiction and irreconcilable incentives and problems.

The advice always builds a model (often true) and uses it to argue what must be done (also true). But both parts are often incomplete and hardly anone can speak in any kind of generality how to solve the mess when multiple such models overlap. Espcially telling is that hardly anyone builds models for what the failure modes are, a taxonomy of messes or a catalogue of combat tactics to apply in a jungle of mixed signals. The advice always defaults to obvious goals, north stars, never revealing how to put a machete to a vine for days without bruning out, organising supply of food and water, managing disease and potential hostiles in the trees.

This article is a discussion of jungle tactics that feed a strategy and commander to which we've lost contact, temporarily. Maybe it never existed, in any case, how do we get to a position where we can regroup and strategise so we stop being lost in swamp without a strategy/exit and reliant on air-dropped supplies.

Examples of wicked problems where we offload reality into a myth of "the rules wont apply to us, we're so special that we can compensate".
- We want to make games but nobody truly understands how or has the taste to find people who do know.
- We can't afford good people, so we need to make due without them.
- We need to sell a story to investors and a different story to players.
- We optimise for comfort and process, we don't make hard calls early, we dont heed our canaries, we repeatedly make the wrong calls at leadership that destroy/waste the potential we once had (expensive detours, not trusting the process)
- Not seeing prototyping and loop/coherence protection as almost the only thing that matters.
- Making things more difficult than they need to be. Not recognising what is affordable with agiven budget. Overscoping.
- Toxic Optimism.
- Hope as a strategy
- Not trusting in people who can see the future based on patterns, extrapolation and funnels.
- Not recognising the normative power of early decisions absent a strong corrective vision (echo.md).
