= Making the Elements of Appeal Operational
:toc:
:toclevels: 2

This addendum is a concise, actionable reference to use alongside the main essay (`wide-appeal.md`). It translates the conceptual axes into observable checks and short design moves you can run in reviews or prototypes. Treat each item as a quick triage: What to look for, what you'll observe, and what to do.

== Inoculations

Lightweight audits and guardrails that keep the design coherent and honest. Each inoculation has three parts: "What", "Tell", and "Do" (concrete next steps).

=== Contract audit inoculation

What:: Every hour of play establishes a contract; the first hour sets the promise.

Tell:: Players say "it gets good after…" or leave before they encounter the mature loop.

Do::
* Draft a one-sentence first-hour promise.
* Prototype a tiny, complete loop that demonstrates that promise.
* Remove or postpone chores that delay the mature loop.

=== Subtraction inoculation

What:: Coherence erodes by accumulation; features can contradict intent.

Tell:: Player discussion reduces to a feature list rather than an experience description.

Do::
* For every new system, run a removal test: if the game's identity survives, drop it.
* Audit overlapping progression systems and merge or remove redundancy.

=== Autopilot inoculation

What:: Long autopilot runs indicate the signature primitive is underused or stakes are muddy.

Tell:: Players call it a "podcast game" or report grinding/turning their brain off.

Do::
* Identify the smallest meaningful choice and increase its informational weight.
* Replace purely numeric goals with choice-weighted outcomes.

=== Friction cost accounting inoculation

What:: Repeated pains must purchase meaning; otherwise they are tax.

Tell:: QoL complaints flood feedback channels; players circumvent systems with guides or exploits.

Do::
* List the top 5 repeated actions per session and classify each as teach/test/express or tax.
* Compress, automate, or remove actions classified as tax.

=== Midgame rot inoculation

What:: Many titles fail in the middle—novelty stalls and padding appears.

Tell:: Consistent quit windows at similar hour ranges.

Do::
* Map player retention by hour and identify plateaus.
* Inject recombinations of existing mechanics to create novelty without new systems.

=== Handle economy inoculation

What:: Too many names and places dilute cultural handles.

Tell:: Players recall the game vaguely or use generic terms a year later.

Do::
* Enforce salience: pick fewer named entities and repeat them with variation and consequence.
* Tie symbols to decisions and rituals rather than decorative lore.

=== Extraction inoculation

What:: Retention tactics can masquerade as conversion.

Tell:: Players return out of obligation (dailies, timers) or anxiety about falling behind.

Do::
* Separate hospitality (easy reattachment) from punishment-for-absence mechanics.
* Remove or refactor timers that punish absence into meaningful re-entry benefits.

== Opportunity generators

Repeatable design patterns that create fitness for loops and habitats. Each generator is phrased as a small, testable prototype idea.

=== Show-the-mature-loop generator

Goal:: Surface the mature loop in the first hour.

How:: Build a compact version of the late-game loop with real feedback and stakes; test new players' comprehension and desire to continue.

=== Competence ladder generator

Goal:: Make progress feel like skill acquisition rather than numeric inflation.

How:: Provide isolated challenges with clear feedback and then recombine skills in layered encounters.

=== Signature compression generator

Goal:: Make the primitive explainable and demonstrate-able.

How:: Design for mimability — if a move can't be mimed or clipped, iterate it until it can.

=== Meaningful constraint generator

Goal:: Use visible limits to make intent felt.

How:: Restrict tools and force tradeoffs that reveal the theme in choices.

=== Hinge planting generator

Goal:: Build recontextualization into the structure, not as an appendage.

How:: Plant seeds early, let a stable mental model form, then introduce a hinge that changes how earlier play is interpreted.

=== Ritual and landmark generator

Goal:: Turn handles into lived rituals.

How:: Create repeatable rituals (prepare, depart, recover, return) and embed landmarks inside them to become cultural anchors.

=== Anecdote engine generator

Goal:: Encourage "I went to do X and then..." stories.

How:: Design for emergent collisions and aftermaths that change player plans and produce short narratives.

=== Returnability generator

Goal:: Lower the cost of reattachment after absence.

How:: Provide quick, meaningful re-entry actions, clear orientation steps, and low re-learning costs; keep hospitality distinct from coercion.

== Capture mechanisms

Name what each mechanism buys and what it costs. Treat capture as engineering tradeoffs rather than moral labels.

=== Cultural capture mechanisms

==== Handle capture

What:: Names, objects, motifs that let people point and remember.

Do:: Tie handles to consequence and ritual; repeat them in decisions.

==== Clip capture

What:: Moments readable at a glance that travel as clips.

Do:: Create moments with clear stakes and visible skill expression.

==== Sentence capture

What:: A single-sentence description that survives compression.

Do:: Distill the legible promise + primitive into one clear line.

==== Hinge capture

What:: Recontextualization moments that reorganize memory.

Do:: Ensure hinges change behavior, not just trivia.

==== Scaffold capture

What:: Community scaffolds that lower literacy barriers.

Do:: Encourage tutorials, challenge runs, and modular mods that expand funnel reach.

=== Behavioral capture mechanisms

==== Loss / behindness capture

What:: Dailies, seasons, and expiring rewards that produce compliance.

Cost:: Trust and long-term recommendation potential.

==== Variable reward capture

What:: Loot volatility that may feel like excitement but obscures stakes.

Cost:: Replaces clear value hierarchies with gambling-like uncertainty.

==== Progress treadmill capture

What:: Numeric progression that substitutes motion for mastery.

Cost:: Motion without meaning.

==== Social obligation capture

What:: Calendared commitments and social pressure.

Do:: Design for community but avoid punishing absence.

==== Friction hostage capture

What:: Monetizing or gating resolved friction.

Cost:: Short-term revenue for long-term reputational harm.

== The operational rule

For every capture mechanism you consider, explicitly note: What it buys, what it costs, and what downstream effects are likely (trust, recommendation language, and the kind of "favorite" produced). Use this as a short table in design reviews.
