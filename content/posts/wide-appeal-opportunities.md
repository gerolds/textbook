This short addendum is meant to be read alongside the main essay `content/posts/wide-appeal.md` — think of it as a practical toolbox that translates the conceptual axes in that piece into observable checks, repeatable design moves, and explicit capture mechanics. Where the main essay explains "what" matters for wide appeal, this document explains "how" to operationalize it in design reviews and prototypes.

## Inoculations

These are lightweight audits and guardrails that prevent the design from drifting into contradiction or self-deception. Each inoculation has a visible tell (what you’ll observe in players or discourse) and a concrete move you can try.

1. Contract audit inoculation
	- What: Every hour of play has a contract; the first hour sets expectations.
	- Tell: Players say “it gets good after…” or they bounce before the mature loop.
	- Move: Write the first-hour promise in plain language, demonstrate the mature loop early, and remove pre-loop chores.

2. Subtraction inoculation
	- What: Coherence dies by accumulation.
	- Tell: Player discourse becomes a feature inventory.
	- Move: Run a removal test — if removing a system doesn't change the game's felt identity, cut it.

3. Autopilot inoculation
	- What: Long autopilot stretches mean the signature primitive is underpowered or stakes are unclear.
	- Tell: Players call it a “podcast game,” report grinding, or say they “turn their brain off.”
	- Move: Deepen the smallest meaningful choice; add informational stakes rather than just bigger numbers.

4. Friction cost accounting inoculation
	- What: Repeated pain must purchase meaning.
	- Tell: QoL complaints dominate discussion; players route around play with guides or exploits.
	- Move: List the top repeated actions, then compress, automate, or remove low-information repetition.

5. Midgame rot inoculation
	- What: Many games fail in the middle, not the opening.
	- Tell: Players quit at the same time window (hours 3–12 depending on scope).
	- Move: Define what should change at key hour marks and recombine mechanics to create novelty without adding whole new systems.

6. Handle economy inoculation
	- What: Too many names and locations create cultural fog.
	- Tell: Players speak vaguely about the world long after release.
	- Move: Enforce salience: reduce entities, repeat them with variation, and tie them to decisions and rituals.

7. Extraction inoculation
	- What: Retention tactics can masquerade as conversion.
	- Tell: Players describe obligation, anxiety about dailies/timers, or returning to avoid falling behind.
	- Move: Separate genuine returnability (hospitality) from punishment-for-absence mechanics.

## Opportunity generators

Patterns that reliably create loop fitness and habitat fitness. These are constructive design moves you can iterate on in prototyping.

1. Show-the-mature-loop generator
	- Build the first hour as a miniature of the whole: real verb, real feedback, real stakes, and a taste of later texture. This is the highest-leverage way to improve legible promise.

2. Competence ladder generator
	- Mastery conversion comes from a ladder where failure is informative. Provide clear feedback, stable rules, and challenges that isolate a skill before recombining them.

3. Signature compression generator
	- Make the primitive describable and showable. If a primitive can't be mimed, it won't travel. Design for explainability without dumbing down.

4. Meaningful constraint generator
	- Coherence strengthens when the game says "no" visibly. Restrict tools, remove redundant systems, and force tradeoffs that express the theme.

5. Hinge planting generator
	- Recontextualization is built, not bolted on: plant seeds early, let a stable model form, then introduce a hinge that changes behavior. The test is whether players play differently after the reveal.

6. Ritual and landmark generator
	- Give players stable rituals (prepare, depart, recover, return) and memorable landmarks embedded inside those rituals. This turns handles into lived practices.

7. Anecdote engine generator
	- Design for emergent collisions and interruptions with aftermath — the things that produce "I went to do X and then…" stories.

8. Returnability generator
	- Make reattachment easy after absence: quick meaningful actions, clear reorientation, and low re-learning cost. Distinguish hospitality from coercion.

## Capture mechanisms

Capture mechanisms explain why certain outputs (shares, retention spikes, clips) appear. Name what each mechanism buys and what it costs; treat them as engineering tradeoffs.

### Cultural capture mechanisms

1. Handle capture
	- Names, objects, places, and motifs that let people point and remember. Effective when tied to consequence and ritual.

2. Clip capture
	- Moments whose stakes and skill are readable at a glance. Clip capture aids transmission but is not the same as depth.

3. Sentence capture
	- A clean one-sentence description that survives compression — a legible promise plus a signature primitive.

4. Hinge capture
	- Recontextualization moments that reorganize memory and make the game a "you must experience it" object.

5. Scaffold capture
	- Community scaffolds (tutorials, challenge runs, mods) that lower the literacy barrier and widen the funnel.

### Behavioral capture mechanisms

1. Loss / behindness capture
	- Dailies, seasons, timers, or expiring rewards that produce compliance rather than affection. Cost: trust.

2. Variable reward capture
	- Loot volatility that feels exciting but can replace stakes clarity with gambling fog.

3. Progress treadmill capture
	- Numeric progression that substitutes motion for mastery.

4. Social obligation capture
	- Calendared commitments and social pressure that can become meaningful community or coercive dependence depending on design.

5. Friction hostage capture
	- Monetizing or gating resolved friction; converts accidental pain into revenue at long-term reputational cost.

## The operational rule

For each capture mechanism you choose, explicitly name what it buys and what it costs (transmission, retention, trust, recommendation language, type of "favorite" produced). If you embed this list in the main essay, the natural placement is after the section on "Operational outputs" and before "Habitat fitness": outputs are what you observe; capture mechanisms explain why those outputs exist.
